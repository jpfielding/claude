# Persuade mode: landing and marketing pages

Secondary mode. Mostly from Hallmark (Nutlope/hallmark) and Taste Skill (Leonxlnx/taste-skill), with their genre-specific absolutes softened into defaults.

## Start from the visitor's job
Decide what the page must get the visitor to do (book, buy, sign up, request access, understand a capability) and end the page on that action. Structure follows that job — not the stock hero → 3 features → logos → pricing → FAQ skeleton. **Mirror test**: if your page would work unchanged for a neighboring product ("swap the name and it still fits"), it's generic; rewrite the parts that would survive the swap.

## Hero
- Headline ≤ ~7 words that says what it is; one supporting sentence ≤ ~15 words saying for whom and the hard fact; 1–2 actions (one primary).
- The whole hero (headline, sentence, primary action) visible at 1280×800 without scrolling.
- One focal visual that shows the real product or real outcome. No generic dashboard mock, no fake browser chrome around a gradient blob.

## Page
- ~6–9 sections, ~450 words total for a typical product page; tech pages ~60–80 words per screen. Cut before adding.
- Each section earns its place by answering an objection the visitor actually has.
- Vary section structure; don't repeat the same card grid three times.
- Real proof only: real customer names/quotes/numbers the user provided. Missing proof → leave the section out or leave a marked slot. (Floor rule.)

## Visual
- Classify the business first; it decides imagery and effects. Software/tech can carry one technical visual moment (a shader, a live product demo). Local/physical businesses get real photography and flat color; WebGL on a bakery site is a tell.
- One authored motion moment per page (see motion.md); hover: cards lift ~2px, images zoom 1.02–1.03 within their frame. No tilt cards, no auto-advancing carousels or timed tabs.
- Larger type scale (1.25–1.333), more whitespace than apps, but the same token discipline.

## Pre-handback self-score (1–5 each; anything < 3 → revise before critique)
- **Specificity** — would this survive the mirror test?
- **Hierarchy** — one obvious first read, one obvious action?
- **Restraint** — is every effect and section earning its place?
- **Execution** — spacing on scale, type on scale, states covered?
- **Honesty** — every claim sourced from the user?
